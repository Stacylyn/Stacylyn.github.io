Finding Her Voice

Stacylyn Bennett-Burnett was apparently born knowing how to take a bow.

According to her mother, at the tender age of three, Stacylyn was attending a rodeo when the clowns came out to entertain the crowd. As the audience erupted in applause, little Stacylyn stood up, waved enthusiastically, and began taking bows. Clearly, she assumed the applause was intended for her.

It was an early indication of things to come.

Born into a military family as the eldest of seven children, Stacylyn spent her childhood moving across America, eventually living in eleven different states.

Her mother, an enthusiastic admirer of the Osmond family, entertained dreams of creating a singing family of her own. With five young children at the time, all under the age of nine, she had plenty of potential performers to work with.

There was just one problem: the other children hated performing.

Stacylyn, on the other hand, loved every minute of it.

Whether singing at community events, family reunions, or church gatherings, she was always ready to take the stage, singing loudly and proudly.

Sadly, she didn't always know the actual words, but that didn't stop her from singing loud!

When Stacylyn was twelve, her grandmother recognized her dramatic abilities and arranged acting lessons with Audra Moss, a former vaudeville actress.

For two years, Stacylyn studied with her, developing the foundations of acting that would eventually take her to New York City. Then, as so often happened in military life, the family moved again.

Throughout her school years, music and performing remained a passion pursued whenever opportunity allowed.

Academic commitments left little room for school theater programs, so church performances became her principal outlet.

But a professional singing career? That wasn't part of the plan.

Her father, a career military man, had a practical philosophy: music wasn't a real job. Therefore, for a time, Stacylyn expected her future to lie elsewhere.
