An Unexpected Scholarship

Upon graduating from high school, Stacylyn learned that her parents couldn't afford to send her to college. The family moved to Texas, where she found work at a grocery store, with little idea of how she might continue her education.

Then, during a church service, she sang.

Among those listening was a visitor… the music director from St. Philip's College. Impressed by her voice, he approached her with an extraordinary offer: a full scholarship to study in the college's Jazz and Theater program.

There was only one complication.

Stacylyn didn't particularly want to major in music. She wanted to study mathematics and computers.

So she accepted the music scholarship on the condition that she could minor in mathematics. Then, demonstrating a determination that would become something of a lifelong characteristic, she marched over to the mathematics department and announced that she wanted to qualify for one of their full scholarships as well.

The department explained that its full scholarships were reserved for promising students entering directly from high school.

Undeterred, Stacylyn enrolled in the music program, pursued her mathematics studies, and made the most of the opportunity. By the end of the year, she had accomplished something rather remarkable: the mathematics department offered her a FULL scholarship.

At the same time, another opportunity appeared — a partial scholarship to the American Musical and Dramatic Academy (AMDA) in New York City.

Now she faced a choice between two very different futures.
