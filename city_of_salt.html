City of Salt Film Summary

A dramatic mystery exploring the devastating consequences of silence, prejudice, and secrets within a seemingly perfect family.

Behind the doors of a beautiful home in an affluent Utah neighborhood lives the Doran family—a successful, respected Latter-day Saint family whose seemingly idyllic life conceals a growing divide.

William Doran is a prominent political figure and favored candidate for the Utah State Senate. But beneath the family's polished public image lie uncomfortable truths, unspoken struggles, and a dangerous unwillingness to confront difficult conversations.

When William and Jessica Doran's teenage daughter, Theo, mysteriously disappears and her bloodied clothing is discovered in the woods, the family's carefully constructed world begins to unravel.

Told through two intertwining timelines, City of Salt follows a determined detective (Kim Stone) investigating Theo's disappearance, while flashbacks reveal the events leading up to the tragedy. As the investigation unfolds, audiences gradually discover the devastating consequences of bullying, stereotyping, intolerance, substance abuse, and the failure to communicate openly within families.

More than a murder mystery, City of Salt is a cautionary tale about the dangers of silence and the importance of allowing young people to embrace their individuality. It challenges families to confront uncomfortable realities, listen without judgment, and recognize that even the most outwardly successful homes can harbor heartbreaking secrets.

Because sometimes the greatest danger to a family isn't what happens outside its walls—but what remains unspoken within them.

My Experience Filming City of Salt

I was filming another project, Angel de la Muerte, with director Oscar Sanchez when he showed me the casting call for City of Salt and suggested I audition. I looked over the available roles and thought I should audition for one of the smaller parts to increase my chances of getting into the film. But Oscar encouraged me to be brave and audition for the lead role of Jessica Doran.

So, I leaned into "bravery" and prepared my audition sides for Jessica. To my astonishment, I was called back for the role!

There were about four or five other Jessica candidates in the room, and we were handed an extremely emotional scene with only a few minutes to prepare. I didn't think I could muster up the grief and tears the scene required, but instead of worrying about whether I could cry, I decided to forget about the tears altogether and just lean into the experience of the scene.

I spoke the words, felt the urgency, and suddenly, right there in front of the director, I was in tears, crying out the words in Jessica's extreme grief. I felt a shift in the room, and later that day, I received a call informing me that I had been cast as Jessica.

Working with Alecia Oberle

Working with director Alecia Oberle was a true pleasure. Alecia is a very emotional person herself, and she has a wonderful ability to direct an actor through the emotions of a scene. We worked really well together.

Alecia had written the screenplay based on an earlier poem she'd written on the subject. There was some drama over what the look and feel of the film should be, but Alecia eventually convinced the producers that her vision was the best direction for the project, and we finally got into filming. And I'm so glad she did, because I think the look of this film is GORGEOUS!

A Seven-Foot Husband and a 5 foot Wife!

One of the challenges of filming involved the lead couple.

Jake Ulasich, who was perfectly cast as William Doran, is over seven feet tall. I was cast as his wife, Jessica, and I'm 5'1". You can imagine the difficulty of getting both our faces into the camera frame for a two-shot!

I constantly wore extremely high heels, and then 4-inch platform shoes. But even that wasn't enough. I was continually being placed on an apple box, up on the second stair of a staircase, or on a much higher mound of dirt—whatever would bring me closer to Jake's height. It worked! And when you watch the film, you'd never guess our extreme height difference. Movie magic!

Crying All Day Long

The cast was magical to work with, and everyone was very supportive of each other.

Except during the funeral scene!

That scene was so heavy that the crew and extras started joking around and trying to make me laugh. It was sweet, really, but it also made it very difficult to remain grief-stricken while Jessica stood at the grave. I understand how difficult it can be for everyone to work around such heavy emotions when they just want to be happy and have a fun day. But somehow, we made it work.

What surprised me most was that I was able to do all my crying scenes for an entire day of filming. I was crying practically all day! I made sure to stay hydrated because I wanted to have enough tears available for every scene. However, I hadn't counted on becoming depleted of electrolytes.

I barely made it through the last shot of the day before nearly passing out! I didn't know about that! Fortunately, it was an easy fix. Someone gave me Gatorade and a cookie, and I revived. Thankfully, we had already filmed all my scenes, and I was gratefully released for the day.

Lesson learned!

The Surprise Ending

I especially love the surprise ending of City of Salt, which isn't even shown until AFTER the end credits!

And filming that final scene turned out to be a surprise for me, too.

The directors filmed me delivering a politician's speech and told me it would simply be used as a silent montage with music playing over it. So, I delivered a speech I had written as if I were running for the Senate myself, never expecting anyone to actually hear it.

But they liked the speech so much that they decided to stop the music and play my speech instead!

That was a wonderful surprise, and I think it made the ending even more impactful.

Releasing a Film During COVID

Unfortunately, City of Salt came out in 2020, just as COVID shut down movie theaters everywhere.

The producers discussed delaying the release, but nobody knew when the worldwide quarantine would be lifted. So they decided to hold a special screening at a Park City theater for friends, family, and donors, followed by a worldwide digital release.

The response was incredible!

The film received multiple nominations and awards, including Best Director (Alecia Oberle), Best Actor (Jake Ulasich), Best Actress (Stacylyn Bennett), Best Youth Actress (Lauren Holdt), Best Editing, Best Script, Best Cinematic Production, and many other awards.

I think Alecia Oberle and John Farmer created a visually stunning film that speaks to people in an unusual and impactful way.

I'm very proud to have been part of City of Salt, not only because of the recognition it received, but because of the important message it carries about families, communication, and the consequences of refusing to talk about uncomfortable things.
